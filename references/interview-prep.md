# Interview-Readiness Brief

A CV wins an **interview**, and the interview re-litigates the CV. Every claim you tailored — especially REFRAMED and AMPLIFIED bullets — is a question the candidate may be asked to defend. A candidate who can't speak fluently to their own CV fails the interview the CV won them. This brief turns the data the skill already holds into a defense the candidate can rehearse.

It is **near-free to produce**: everything below already exists by the time the judges pass — the claim-provenance map (every reframed claim traces to a real source), the judges' `SUPPLEMENTARY_QUESTIONS_FOR_CANDIDATE`, and the HONEST-GAPS list. Surfacing it is the highest-value, lowest-cost deliverable in the whole flow — don't throw it away at the end.

## What to produce

Emit a short brief (in the application language) with three parts:

### 1. Be ready to explain (your tailored claims)

For each **REFRAMED** or **AMPLIFIED** claim on the final CV, list:
- the **claim as written** on the CV,
- the **real source fact** it traces to (from the provenance checkpoint), and
- a one-line **"be ready to explain…"** prompt.

> Example
> - CV says: *"Built and maintained CI/CD pipelines, cutting deploy time 40%."*
>   Source: you wrote automated deployment scripts at Role B; the 40% is your confirmed number.
>   Be ready to: walk through what the scripts did end-to-end, which tool, and how you measured the 40%.

The point is not to coach a story — it's to make sure every word on the page is something the candidate can stand behind truthfully and specifically. If a candidate *can't* comfortably explain a claim, that's a signal the reframing went too far — soften it back.

### 2. Honest gaps — your answer if asked

For each **HONEST-GAP** that remains (a must-have the candidate genuinely doesn't meet), give the honest, forward-looking framing to use *if the interviewer raises it* — the same framing chosen in the tailoring plan (transferable strength + learning trajectory). Never a script to dodge; a true, confident answer.

> Example: *"I haven't worked in fintech directly, but my analytics work was on 10M+ monthly e-commerce transactions — high-volume transactional data, funnel integrity, anomaly detection — which transfers closely. I'm ramping on the domain-specific regulation."*

### 3. Questions the screen already raised

Carry over the judges' `SUPPLEMENTARY_QUESTIONS_FOR_CANDIDATE` (Recruiter logistics — work authorization, relocation, availability; Hiring Manager — scope/seniority clarifications). These are the exact questions a real recruiter or manager flagged, so they are the likeliest to come up. List them with the candidate's honest answer where known.

## Rules

- **Honest only.** This brief never invents a story; it points each CV claim back to a true source and prepares a truthful answer. If a claim has no defensible source, the fix is to change the CV, not to coach a cover story.
- **Keep it thin.** This is a defense brief generated from data you already hold — not a mock-interview module. A page or less.
- **Flag the over-reach signal.** If preparing the brief surfaces a claim the candidate cannot truthfully defend, treat it as a tailoring error and walk the claim back on the CV.
