About the team
The Image Reconstruction group builds the software that turns acquired data into
the images a clinician reads. We sit between the physicists who design sequences
and the platform teams who ship them, and we own the reconstruction path from
raw acquisition through to the images that leave the system. The group is eleven
engineers and three physicists, working in two-week iterations, and every change
that reaches a product goes through the same review and qualification path.

Responsibilities
- Own and extend the production reconstruction pipeline, in C++, on Linux.
- Profile and optimise GPU kernels; the reconstruction budget per study is fixed
  and every new feature has to fit inside it.
- Work directly with physicists to bring new sequences into product, from a
  research prototype to something that runs unattended on a scanner.
- Review your colleagues' code and have yours reviewed; we do not merge without
  a second reader.
- Mentor engineers joining the group, and write the design notes that let the
  next person change your code safely.
- Contribute to release qualification: reproduce the regression suite, triage
  what moved, and sign off on what you understand.

- Five or more years of professional C++ in a product codebase.
- Hands-on GPU programming, CUDA or an equivalent.
- A working understanding of MRI or CT physics — enough to argue with a
  physicist about an artefact and be useful in the argument.
- Experience working in a Linux HPC environment: batch schedulers, remote
  debugging, reproducible builds.
- Fluent English, written and spoken.

Nice to have

- Python tooling for analysis and test automation.

What we value
Ownership, evidence over opinion, and engineers who can explain their work to
